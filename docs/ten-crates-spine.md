# The 10 Crates

The 10 crates are not ordinary modules.

They are the minimum body of LogLine in Rust:

```text
who
did
this
when
confirmed_by
if_ok
if_doubt
if_not
status
logline-cli
```

Nine are runtime anatomy. One is the shell.

```text
who through status = body
logline-cli        = mouth
```

## The Spine

The body is deliberately thin.

That is not a lack of ambition. It is the design.

A thin spine can receive:

```text
domain vocabulary
industry policy
evidence adapters
identity systems
receipt stores
ledger backends
LLM workflows
small operational grammars
large institutional governance
```

without changing the body.

The body must remain:

```text
who did this when confirmed_by if_ok if_doubt if_not status
```

## What Each Crate Protects

### `who`

Protects accountable origin.

It asks:

```text
who carries this?
```

It may classify origins, but it must not confirm evidence or select a branch.

### `did`

Protects named movement.

It asks:

```text
what act or operation is being named?
```

It may classify verbs, but it must not execute them.

### `this`

Protects bounded matter.

It asks:

```text
what is the object, claim, target, payload, or matter?
```

It may locate and type matter, but it must not fetch the world by itself.

### `when`

Protects time, phase, and simulation conditions.

It asks:

```text
when or under what temporal condition is this valid?
```

It may simulate time, but it must not schedule consequence.

### `confirmed_by`

Protects the evidence pivot.

It asks:

```text
what would make this admissible?
```

It is the only slot that collapses evidence into:

```text
ok
doubt
not
```

It must not invent proof.

### `if_ok`

Protects real release.

It asks:

```text
what may happen if evidence is sufficient?
```

It names release. It does not execute by itself.

### `if_doubt`

Protects possible-world simulation.

It asks:

```text
what should happen while this is unresolved?
```

It may ask, suspend, dispatch, clarify, or simulate.

It must not execute consequence.

### `if_not`

Protects falsification and rejection.

It asks:

```text
what happens if this is false, forbidden, or inadmissible?
```

It records the no. It is not a convenience fallback.

### `status`

Protects closure.

It asks:

```text
what is this allowed to become after the governed walk?
```

It closes lifecycle truth from walked evidence.

### `logline-cli`

Protects the mouth.

It asks:

```text
how does a user or tool speak to the canon?
```

It may parse, print, and dispatch.

It must not become runtime anatomy.

## Why This Can Scale

The 10 crates are small because scale should arrive as clothing, not surgery.

Different deployments may add:

```text
healthcare evidence adapters
finance release policies
notarial receipts
governance quorum rules
LLM pocket workflows
offline verification stores
high-throughput ledgers
domain-specific route vocabularies
```

But those things must attach to the same slots.

That is the scaling rule:

```text
change the clothing
change the pressure
change the adapters
change the domain
do not change the spine
```

## The Impact

A normal runtime says:

```text
give me input and I will run code
```

LogLine says:

```text
place the claim in a 9-slot body
walk its accountability
test its evidence
simulate its doubt
release only what may be released
record what history is allowed to say happened
```

That is the difference.
